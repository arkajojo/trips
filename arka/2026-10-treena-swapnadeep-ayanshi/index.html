Three things that shape the plan.

  • They land at 6.30am on a Sunday off a red‑eye, so Day 1 is Jewel, a nap, and an easy Orchard Road stroll with all of you — the first real sightseeing starts Monday.
  • Halloween Horror Nights runs at Universal on 22–24 and 29–31 Oct: not for under‑13s and the park closes early on those nights. Universal is therefore Mon 26 Oct, a full‑hours weekday, and the daytime "Trick or Thrills" family Halloween (running until 1 Nov) means Ayanshi still gets her Halloween.
  • Both departures are the 8.40pm SQ516 from Terminal 3, so each Saturday's plan finishes at Changi by 6pm with time for Jewel on the way through.

  • Baggage: 25kg checked each, both ways — room for the shopping.

  • Treena's return fare is Economy Lite (seat selection chargeable, no changes); Swapnadeep's is Economy Value (changeable for a fee). Worth knowing only if plans shift.

Before they fly

  • Visa. Indian passport holders need a Singapore visa before travel — no visa on arrival. Apply through an ICA‑authorised visa agent in India; it's usually issued as an e‑visa within a few working days, but apply now. Confirm current rules on ica.gov.sg.
  • SG Arrival Card. Free, mandatory, filed online at eservices.ica.gov.sg within 3 days before arrival — so on or after Thu 15 Oct. One per person including Ayanshi. Screenshot the acknowledgement; it also unlocks the automated immigration lanes.
  • Passports valid at least six months beyond 31 Oct.
  • Travel insurance with medical cover.
  • Phones: install Grab and Google Maps before flying; Arka has the SIMs and transport cards covered, so they're connected from the arrival hall.
  • Heights: measure Ayanshi in shoes and note it — ride and go‑kart minimums are in centimetres here (1.00 / 1.07 / 1.22 / 1.30 are the ones that matter).

Getting around on your own

Arka will hand each of you a loaded EZ‑Link card: tap in and out at MRT gates, tap on and off buses. Ayanshi needs her own card (free travel is only for under‑7s). Top‑ups are at any station machine or 7‑Eleven. Google Maps gives door‑to‑door MRT and bus directions here, including which exit to use; trains run roughly 5.30am to midnight, every station has lifts and toilets, and everything is air‑conditioned.

Using Google Maps here (weekdays, on your own)

  • Every day below ends with a Pins list. Tap Map to see where it is, or Go to open transit directions from wherever you're standing — Maps knows Singapore's MRT, LRT and every bus route, with live timings.
  • In directions, pick the train icon (transit). Maps names the line, the platform direction, the number of stops and, at the end, the station exit letter — follow that letter on the yellow signs inside the station.
  • Tap Depart at to plan the evening trips: last trains leave the city around 11.30pm–midnight.
  • Before Day 2, download an offline map of Singapore (Maps → your photo → Offline maps) so it works in the MRT tunnels and in the MacRitchie forest.
  • Save each day's pins to a Maps list ("Singapore trip") the night before; then you just tap the next star.
  • When walking looks long or it's pouring, the Grab app will show a fare in seconds — Arka's card is on the account.

Grab is the Uber here: use GrabCar for the three of you when it rains, when it's late, or when you're carrying beach bags. Sentosa and Mandai each have their own last‑mile link, described on the day.

Rules that catch visitors out. No eating or drinking on trains and buses, including water. Vapes and e‑cigarettes are illegal to bring in or hold. No chewing gum. Jaywalking, littering and smoking outside marked zones are fined. Drug offences carry the death penalty; never carry anything for anyone. Tap water is safe to drink. Tipping isn't expected.

Day by day

Anything tagged Optional can be dropped without losing the day — if they're tired, drop it. Every day has a rain plan, because October afternoons often bring a one‑hour thunderstorm. Where you're on your own, the transport line tells you the station and exit; on weekend days Arka drives.

19.00

Rain plan: Jewel and Orchard are indoors end to end. Nothing to change.

2Monday 19 Oct — Marina Bay, all of it in one dayMerlion · Cloud Forest & Flower Dome · Skyway · Future World · SkyPark deck at sunset · Garden Rhapsody · Spectra
Get there: EWNS Raffles Place, Exit H, 8 minutes' walk along the river past the Fullerton Hotel to the Merlion. Later in the day everything is a walk apart; DTCC Bayfront is the station home.
08.45

Merlion Park — the first skyline photos while the light is soft and the crowds thin. Then the waterfront loop: past the Esplanade, over the Helix Bridge (Ayanshi finds the DNA letters lit into it) and along the Marina Bay Sands boardwalk to Gardens by the Bay. Thirty minutes with stops.

10.00

Cloud Forest first while it's cool — the 35‑metre indoor waterfall, the Cloud Walk skybridge, the Lost World at the top — then the Flower Dome. About 2.5 hours for both. Then the OCBC Skyway, 22 metres up between the Supertrees.

13.00
Lunch: Satay by the Bay, the hawker centre inside the Gardens — satay, BBQ stingray, a plate of char kway teow, sugarcane juice — or Rasapura Masters in the Marina Bay Sands basement for air‑con.

17.45

Sands SkyPark Observation Deck — the deck on top of the "ship", 57 floors up. Book the 5.45 entry so you get the city in daylight, the sunset around 6.55, and the lights coming on below. The Flyer is not needed; this is the view.

19.15
Dinner: Makansutra Gluttons Bay beside the Esplanade — open‑air hawker stalls with the skyline behind. Fried carrot cake (a radish omelette), oyster omelette, Hokkien mee, BBQ chicken wings.
20.45

Garden Rhapsody at the Supertree Grove — free, 15 minutes, the 8.45pm show. Lie flat on the grass under the trees. Then 10 minutes' walk to the Event Plaza in front of Marina Bay Sands for Spectra, the free water‑laser‑fountain show at 9pm (or catch the 7.45 Rhapsody and the 8pm Spectra and eat afterwards — either order works).

Ayanshi's moment: her drawing swimming across a wall, then a forest of glowing trees singing at night.
Getting home: DTCC Bayfront, under the Shoppes, 5 minutes from Event Plaza.

Rain plan: the domes, the museum, the Shoppes and the deck are indoors; the light shows run unless there's lightning, and repeat every night if you miss them.

3Tuesday 20 Oct — Mandai: Zoo by day, safari by nightSingapore Zoo · River Wonders · Night Safari
Get there: NS Khatib, Exit A → Mandai Khatib Shuttle (tap EZ‑Link, every 10–20 min, 15 min ride). Leave home by 8 to walk in when gates open at 8.30.

Singapore Zoo, open‑concept, no cages. In order: the Fragile Forest walkthrough (lemurs and flying foxes at arm's length), the orangutan boardwalk, the giraffe and elephant feeding sessions (times on the board, usually late morning), the Splash Safari sea‑lion show around 10.30 and Rainforest Fights Back around 12.30. Ride the tram once to save legs; Rainforest Kidzworld has a water‑play area if Ayanshi needs to cool off (swimwear under clothes).

4Wednesday 21 Oct — Tiong Bahru, the museum, then Chinatown after darkArt‑deco estate · market breakfast · Tiong Bahru Bakery · National Museum · temple at dusk · VR · roast duck
Get there: EW Tiong Bahru, Exit A, 5 minutes to the market.
09.00
Second breakfast at Tiong Bahru Market (the hawker floor upstairs): Jian Bo chwee kueh (steamed rice cakes with preserved radish — the stall that made them famous), 178 lor mee, and a kopi. Locals only, at this hour.

Then the Tiong Bahru walk: Singapore's oldest housing estate, 1930s art‑deco blocks with rounded balconies and spiral back stairs, the wartime air‑raid shelter under Block 78, the Yip Yew Chong murals (a bird‑singing corner, a pasar, a fortune teller) on Seng Poh, Eng Hoon and Eu Chin Streets, the Qi Tian Gong monkey‑god temple, the little independent shops on Yong Siak Street. An hour, flat, mostly shaded.

11.00
End at Tiong Bahru Bakery on Eng Hoon Street — the original branch: the kouign‑amann, a pain au chocolat, and a cold brew at the pavement tables.
12.30

National Museum of Singapore (NE Dhoby Ghaut, Exit A, or CC Bras Basah; 5 minutes' walk). The Singapore History Gallery does 700 years in an hour with a good audio guide; upstairs, Story of the Forest is a walk‑through digital animation of 19th‑century natural‑history drawings Ayanshi will want to do twice. Lunch at the museum café or Food Opera at ION on the way if the bakery wasn't enough.

15.00

OptionalTwo hours at home to rest — Chinatown is better after four anyway. Or stay on and see the rotating exhibition.

16.15

Chinatown, NEDT Chinatown, Exit A, onto Pagoda Street. Go straight to the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple before it closes at 5 (free; shoulders and knees covered; ask for the rooftop orchid garden and the giant prayer wheel), then Sri Mariamman next door, the oldest Hindu temple in the city — worth two minutes for its gopuram alone.

Shopping as the lanterns come on: the Pagoda Street and Trengganu Street stalls for magnets, silk, batik and cheap Halloween costume bits; Yue Hwa Chinese Products on Eu Tong Sen Street (five floors of tea, silk, jade, calligraphy sets — the proper emporium, open till 9); the Chinatown Complex market for dried goods and teas to carry home. Haggle gently on the street, never in Yue Hwa.

18.15

The VR arena in Chinatown — Arka is booking a session (60–90 minutes; three players is a perfect team; trainers, not sandals). Ayanshi's first full‑body VR: expect her to talk about nothing else at dinner.

20.00
Dinner: Noodle King for the roast duck (Arka's pick — he'll send the address). Back‑up: the Chinatown Complex Food Centre upstairs on Smith Street, the biggest hawker centre in Singapore — Hawker Chan's soya chicken, claypot rice, a plate of char kway teow — and, before home, a walk up lantern‑lit Ann Siang Hill and Club Street, the prettiest two streets in the city after dark.
Ayanshi's moment: the kouign‑amann, then VR, then lanterns.

Rain plan: the museum and Yue Hwa swallow any storm; the Tiong Bahru walk has covered five‑foot ways the whole route.

One MRT stop or a five‑minute drive to CC Labrador Park. Walk the Berlayer Creek mangrove boardwalk down to the sea, then Labrador Nature Reserve: the little beach and jetty facing Sentosa, the old British gun emplacements, kingfishers and monitor lizards in the trees. It's the quietest coast in the city and the light after 4pm is gold.

16.00

Weather and what to pack

Late October is the turn into the north‑east monsoon: 25–33°C, 80%+ humidity, sunny mornings and a fast, loud thunderstorm most afternoons that clears within the hour. Dress for Kolkata in September and you'll be right.

  • Light cotton/linen clothes; one set that covers shoulders and knees for the temples and the mosque
  • Comfortable walking shoes that survive getting wet (Saturday 24 is about 8km on foot), plus sandals; trainers for VR and go‑karts
  • Swimwear ×2 each, quick‑dry towel, rash guard for Ayanshi
  • Small fold‑up umbrella and a poncho
  • Sunscreen SPF50, hats, sunglasses, insect repellent (MacRitchie, the ridge walk, the Night Safari)
  • Type G (UK three‑pin) adapter — Indian plugs don't fit
  • Power bank; you'll be on Maps and Grab all day
  • Refillable water bottles
  • A light layer for the aggressive mall and MRT air‑con, and for Snow City
  • Basic medicines; Guardian and Watsons pharmacies are in every mall for anything else
